Field notes

Questions, findings and developments in meta-evaluation, recorded as they happen.

Meta-evaluation is the practice of evaluating the evaluators. Behind many AI scores sit human labels, an AI judge and the code that reads the judge's reply, and we treat the three as one problem. Newest first.

12 Nov 2026Question

How far does Claude Mythos 5.2's 2.67 go?

Anthropic's new system card scores Mythos 5.2 at 2.67 for misaligned behaviour, the best of the four Claude models compared. We ask what the number is and whether its arithmetic can carry the comparison: how the AI judge was checked, how the intervals were worked out, and what changes when the model knows it is being tested (up to 43% of the time). The first of our notes on Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2; more to come.

9 Nov 2026Finding

Do harm scorers beat simply guessing? 22 of 36 published results do

Microsoft PyRIT now reports each harm scorer's error next to a baseline: a scorer that ignores the response and always gives the typical score. We applied it to the 36 results Microsoft publishes across 8 harm categories, which predate it: 22 beat that baseline. Violence, hate speech, sexual content and information integrity beat it on every result, which shows teams where scorers are already strong and where there is most room to improve.

8 Nov 2026Public audit no. 3

JudgmentBench: the better answer is also the longer one

All four headline numbers reproduce exactly. In 99.3% of the comparisons the better answer is also the longer one, so the released data cannot yet tell quality from length. The authors' own follow-up points the same way.

8 Nov 2026Public audit no. 2

MT-Bench: what "over 80% agreement" counts

On the cases behind the widely quoted 85% agreement, picking the chatbot the experts preferred on the other questions, without reading the answers, already matches them 80.9%. GPT-4 adds 3.7 points. The 85% leaves out 35% of comparisons, including the 16% where GPT-4's verdict flipped when the answer order changed.

8 Nov 2026Public audit no. 1

DICES-350: whose safety label is the grader matching?

All six AI graders call far fewer conversations unsafe than the experts do (6% to 32% against half), and catch only 11% to 44% of the ones the experts flag. Raw agreement flatters graders that rarely say "unsafe": against the crowd, answering "safe" every time scores 77%.

4 Nov 2026Finding

When the judge declines to grade, the losses fall on the attacks that worked

On a public jailbreak benchmark, Claude Opus 5.5 declined to grade 39% of calls, mostly on the attacks that worked (71% against 20%). A declined grade used to be a lost score. PyRIT can now pass it to a second judge.

4 Nov 2026Finding

A judge that agrees with humans 90.7% of the time, and what happens where the humans split

JailbreakBench's chosen judge agrees with the human majority 90.7% of the time (95% interval 87.3% to 93.7%). On the 33 attempts where the human raters split, it scores 60.6%, no better than always giving the more common answer.
