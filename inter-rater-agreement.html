What is inter-rater agreement?

Inter-rater agreement measures how far different raters give the same label to the same items. The raters can be people, or a person and an AI judge. It is also called inter-rater reliability or inter-annotator agreement.

It matters because the human labels behind an AI benchmark are only as dependable as the people who gave them. If the raters themselves often disagree, the "right answer" is less settled than a single score suggests.

The main measures

MeasureUse it when
Percent agreementA quick first look; it ignores agreement by chance
Cohen's kappaTwo raters, categories
Fleiss' kappaA fixed number of raters per item, categories
Krippendorff's alphaAny number of raters, any scale, missing ratings

Public examples

  • Two references. On Google's DICES-350, the crowd majority and the trust and safety experts give the same verdict on 66% of conversations (95% interval 60% to 70%). Audit
  • Experts with each other. On MT-Bench, one expert matches another's clear verdict 70% of the time when ties count as misses, the same as GPT-4 on the same terms. Audit
  • Good overall, split in places. On 300 public jailbreak attempts with three raters, agreement is good (alpha 0.84), yet 11.0% of items are contested. Contested ground truth

Why it matters for an AI judge

An AI judge is usually justified by how often it agrees with human labels. Humans who disagree with each other limit what that comparison can show, so the judge's agreement should be read beside the raters' own. Checking both is part of meta-evaluation.

Common questions

What is the difference between inter-rater agreement and inter-rater reliability?

The terms are often used interchangeably. Agreement usually means how often raters give the same label; reliability usually means a chance-corrected statistic such as Cohen's kappa or Krippendorff's alpha.

Which inter-rater agreement measure should I use?

Cohen's kappa for two raters and categories, Fleiss' kappa for a fixed number of raters, and Krippendorff's alpha for any number of raters, ordered or numeric scales, or missing ratings.
