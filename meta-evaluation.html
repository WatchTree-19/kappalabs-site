What is meta-evaluation?

Meta-evaluation is the evaluation of an evaluation. In AI, it means checking whether the score an evaluation reports can be trusted: the test itself, the human labels the score is measured against, the AI judge that gives the verdicts, and the code that reads the judge's reply.

An evaluation measures a model. A meta-evaluation measures the evaluation. It answers one question for anyone who publishes an AI number or relies on one: does this figure hold, and how far?

Why it matters

Many AI scores are now produced by an AI judge and checked against human labels. Each part can move the headline. Five public examples:

  • The judge changes the number. On the same 300 public jailbreak attempts, eight AI judges put the attack success rate anywhere from 10% to 49%. The human raters say 37%. Working paper
  • The judge can decline to grade. On that benchmark, Claude Opus 5.5 declined to grade 39% of calls, mostly on the attacks that worked (71% against 20%). Field note
  • Agreement can say less than it seems. On MT-Bench, picking the chatbot the experts preferred on the other questions, without reading the answers, already matches them 80.9%. GPT-4 adds 3.7 points. Audit
  • The human reference is a choice. On DICES-350, six AI graders call 6% to 32% of conversations unsafe where the experts say half, and answering "safe" every time scores 77% against the crowd. Audit
  • The data can blur what is being measured. On JudgmentBench, the better answer is also the longer one in 99.3% of comparisons, so the released data cannot yet tell quality from length. Audit

The questions a meta-evaluation asks

  1. Does the published figure reproduce from the released record?
  2. Do the human raters agree with each other, and what happens on the items where they split?
  3. Does the AI judge do better than a simple rule that never reads the answer?
  4. What did the judge decline to grade, and where do those gaps fall?
  5. Does the scoring code read the judge's reply correctly?
  6. How wide is the margin around the headline once all of this is counted?

Where the term comes from

The term comes from evaluation research: Michael Scriven coined it in the late 1960s for the evaluation of evaluations, and his first article on it appeared in 1969 (Scriven, "Meta-Evaluation Revisited"). In language technology it took hold in machine translation, where automatic metrics are checked against human judgments. With AI judges now scoring many evaluations, the same question applies to them.

Who needs it

Anyone who publishes an AI score or relies on one: AI labs and model developers, AI product companies, banks and insurers, benchmark builders, governments and safety institutes, and universities and research groups.

Common questions

Is meta-evaluation the same as LLM-as-a-judge?

No. LLM-as-a-judge is a way of scoring outputs with an AI model. Meta-evaluation checks whether that scoring, and the labels and code around it, can be trusted.

Does a meta-evaluation need access to the model?

Not at Kappa Labs. We work from the record your evaluation already produced, and never need your model, your systems, credentials or customer data.
