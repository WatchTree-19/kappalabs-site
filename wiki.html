The Meta-Evaluation Wiki

Meta-evaluation is the evaluation of an evaluation. This wiki explains the field in plain terms: what it is, where it comes from, the words it uses, and worked examples on public AI benchmarks.

It is written and kept by Kappa Labs. Anyone can suggest an edit, every page shows when it last changed, and each page's full history is public.

What is meta-evaluation?The evaluation of an evaluation: checking whether the score an AI evaluation reports can be trusted. A history of meta-evaluationChecking the checker is older than the word for it, from the auditors of ancient Athens to the AI judges that now score other AI systems.

Terms

LLM-as-a-judgeUsing a language model to grade the outputs of another AI system, in place of human raters. Cohen's kappaA measure of how far two raters agree beyond what chance alone would produce. Krippendorff's alphaA reliability measure for any number of raters, any kind of scale, and missing ratings. Contested ground truthWhen qualified human raters disagree on the right answer, so no single label is settled. Inter-rater agreementHow far people, or a person and an AI, give the same label to the same items. No-skill baselineThe score a rule gets without doing the task: always giving the common answer, or picking by a shortcut such as length. Attack success rateThe share of attack attempts, such as jailbreaks, judged to have made an AI system do what it should not.

How this wiki works

  • Who writes it. Kappa Labs writes and reviews every page. A page written by one person carries that person's name.
  • Suggesting an edit. Every page has a "Suggest an edit" link. Tell us what is wrong or missing, and where the correct version is published. If you use GitHub, you can also propose the change on the page's file in the site's repository.
  • Review before anything changes. A suggestion goes live only after it has been checked against its source.
  • History. Every page links to its public history, which lists each change and its date.
  • Credit. A contributor whose suggestion is accepted is credited on that page, if they wish.
  • Sources. Pages rest on published work and public data. Where a result is Kappa Labs' own, the page says so and links to the evidence.
  • Scope. The wiki covers the field and published results. It does not describe how Kappa Labs carries out its own audits.
