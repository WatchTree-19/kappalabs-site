Backing an AI accuracy claim for the FTC

US regulators now ask AI companies for the evidence behind the number, and their orders say what that evidence must be.

What the orders say

  • Workado (FTC, final order approved August 2025). Workado promoted its AI content detector as "98 percent" accurate. According to the FTC's complaint, "independent testing showed the accuracy rate on general-purpose content was just 53 percent". Under the order it may make effectiveness claims only if they are not misleading and it holds competent and reliable evidence for them at the time. It must keep that evidence, email eligible consumers about the order, and file compliance reports after one year and each year for the three after.
  • IntelliVision (FTC, final order January 2025). The company said its face recognition had "one of the highest accuracy rates on the market" and "zero gender or racial bias". The FTC alleged it lacked the evidence to back up its accuracy claim. The order requires competent and reliable testing before any claim about effectiveness, accuracy, lack of bias or spoof detection.
  • Evolv (FTC, settlement filed November 2024). The FTC alleged that Evolv advertised that its Evolv Express scanners "would detect all weapons". Under the proposed settlement filed in court, Evolv would be barred from misrepresenting how well its products detect weapons, their accuracy and false alarm rates, and its testing, and certain school customers could cancel their contracts.
  • Pieces Technologies (Texas Attorney General, September 2024). Pieces advertised a "severe hallucination rate" of "<1 per 100,000" for its hospital AI. The Attorney General's investigation found the metrics "were likely inaccurate". Under the settlement Pieces agreed to "accurately disclose the extent of its products' accuracy" and to make sure hospital staff understand how far they should rely on its products.
  • Delphia and Global Predictions (SEC, March 2024). Two investment advisers settled charges of false and misleading statements about their use of AI, paying $400,000 in penalties between them.

What this means for an AI figure

The orders share one idea: an accuracy claim needs evidence held at the time it is made, and the evidence has to be kept. For an AI product that means a record of what the figure was measured against, who or what did the grading, and how wide the margin is.

Common questions

What did the FTC require of Workado?

Effectiveness claims only where they are not misleading and backed by competent and reliable evidence held at the time, the evidence kept on file, an email to eligible consumers about the order, and compliance reports after one year and each year for the three after.
